# 0006 · Design rules are defaults; the owner's taste binds

## Context
The carta-design skill started as a strict two-colour, no-gradient, quiet-motion system. On 2026-10-30 (T878) the owner rejected two careful, rule-abiding designs as flat ("more modern, add more design, visually top notch") and asked for more freedom. Over many reviews (T743 to T1044) the same taste notes came back, and ignoring them cost a redo round each time.

## Options considered
1. **Keep the look rules as bans.** Safe, but produces screens the owner rejects.
2. **Drop the system.** Loses accessibility and honesty guarantees.
3. **Look rules become defaults; a short list of hard rules and the owner's taste bind.**

## Decision
We chose **option 3**. The carta-design skill wins over other design sources, but its look rules are defaults: colour worlds, gradients, authored diagrams, richer motion and bolder type are allowed where they make a screen modern and polished. These always bind:
- Accessibility: contrast 4.5:1, 44 px targets, reduced motion.
- Honest numbers: data must be valid or it does not show; a missing value gets a "Not yet" placeholder slot, never a made-up number, and stays out of every sum.
- Copy rules: no em or en dashes, six languages, licensed photography only.

The owner's taste, learned across reviews:
- No sublines, tips or method text; no catalogue counts as page furniture.
- No white boxes, white bands or hairlines behind content.
- Prices prominent; the umber cost card is liked, other busy brown blocks are not.
- One typeface, Figtree; no serif, no eyebrows above headings.
- Decisive and clean: one screen, doors with icons and no subtext. Ratings are an ink figure plus a star.
- When the owner sends reference screenshots, copy their layout closely in Carta's colours, type and icons, never the other app's logo or wording.
- Desktop should feel like Airbnb, Booking, Wanderlog; the phone top like Airbnb or Polarsteps.

## Consequences
- Good: designs aim at what the owner approves; the hard rules keep them accessible and honest.
- Bad: more judgement per screen; each visual report says what departed from the defaults.

## How we'll know it works
Fewer redo rounds on owner reviews.
