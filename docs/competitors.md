# Carta competitor map

Compiled 2026-10-21. A list of the products a Carta traveller could use instead of Carta, or next to it, grouped by the job they do. Every row links to the product's own site.

How to read the "Overlap" column, measured against what Carta does (a daily ground cost per destination on a map, then a trip planner and an hour-by-hour day planner, hiking, cycling and beach layers, Europe only, no booking):

- **High**: does the same job for the same traveller. Study closely.
- **Medium**: covers one or two of Carta's surfaces well.
- **Low**: a neighbour the traveller uses before or after Carta, and a source of ideas or hand-off partners.

Links were checked against memory and a light web search. Rows marked *(verify)* changed name or status recently and should be opened before you rely on them.

## What the map shows

1. **Nobody else puts the cost of the days on the map.** The price maps (section 1) price the ticket; the cost sites (section 2) price the day but as a list per city, with no map, no inputs (origin, dates, group, lifestyle) and no hand-off into a plan. That gap is Carta's mission statement, and the search for a tool that combines flight and ground cost on one map found none.
2. **AI planners are the crowded middle.** Mindtrip, Layla, Wonderplan, Wanderlog and now Polarsteps all draft itineraries. Their weak points in reviews are generic or touristy suggestions and prices they cannot back. Carta's day planner competes here on sourced figures, not on chat.
3. **The Dutch and German players are the nearest neighbours.** Polarsteps, Stippl, Lambus, Komoot and Outdooractive are European, map-led, and already in the launch audience's phone. Their pricing (Lambus pay per trip €9.99 or €24.99 yearly) is the reference point for Carta's pass.
4. **Hikers already have Komoot and AllTrails.** Carta's angle for them is the town: what a night and a day of eating cost at the start of the walk, which neither app answers.

## Suggested next steps for the analysis

- Install the High rows (about 20) and walk the same task in each: "a weekend walking trip from Brussels under €300 per person".
- Note for each: how it shows price, what it marks as an estimate, what it charges, and where it hands off.
- Read the 1 to 3 star app store reviews of Wanderlog, Polarsteps, Mindtrip, Komoot and Budget Your Trip: they list what travellers miss.
