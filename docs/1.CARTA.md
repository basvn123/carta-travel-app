# Carta

**A price transparency tool for budget travel in Europe.** Give it your dates and your departure airport, and it prices the entire trip for 1,570 destinations in 43 countries: the flight, the cabin bag, the transfer from the airport, the bed, the food, the local transport. Then it plans the trip city by city and each day hour by hour.

Live at [carta-europetravel.com](https://carta-europetravel.com). The technical layout of this repository is in [README.md](../README.md); this document explains what has been built and why it can be trusted.

## The one rule everything follows

Carta's only real asset is trust in its numbers, so every price on every surface obeys the same chain:

**real harvested quote, then cached third-party quote, then model estimate, and never a blank.**

Each step is labelled for what it is. A harvested fare shows its source and its age ("seen 3 days ago"). A cached quote carries its expiry. An estimate is prefixed with a tilde, tagged "est.", and never dressed up as a bookable price. Every external booking link warns that prices may have changed. The pipeline enforces this end to end: provenance fields travel from the harvesters through the merge into the wire format and onto every pin, row, receipt line and detail sheet.

One consequence worth spelling out: an estimate is only allowed to exist where a flight verifiably exists. The four directly harvested carriers publish complete calendars, so a missing day genuinely means they do not fly. An estimated price therefore ships only for route-months where the Aviasales cache proves some other airline (Brussels Airlines, Transavia, TUI fly, Norwegian, ...) actually serves that route in that month. No evidence, no estimate, and the trip honestly falls back to the road.

## What the traveller gets

- **The price map.** Every destination pinned with its real total for your dates, your group size and your lifestyle, rendered as WebGL layers that stay smooth at catalogue scale. The cheapest option wears the only yellow flag on the map. Filters compose: price window, country, trip kinds, rating band, hidden gems, UNESCO, top beaches, and "reachable within 3/5/8/12 hours by train or bus" where travel-time data exists.
- **The receipt.** Carta's signature element. Every total is itemised line by line (flight out, flight back, bag, transfer, stay, food, local transport) with nothing rounded to look tidier, because €24.99 is the point and €25 is a different product.
- **The trip planner.** Multi-city routes with mode-aware legs: every train, bus, ferry and drive priced through one resolver, with sea crossings, tolls, vignettes, fuel from your own car model, and Omio or operator links per leg.
- **The day planner.** A map with the day drawn on it, and three tabs under it: today's plan (the places in order, and nothing else), add more (whole ready-made days, or a search and browse over the catalogue of 134,657 points of interest, 80,457 with photographs), and files (the traveller's own tickets and confirmations, kept on their device, plus a note for the group). Dwell times, walking budgets and must-see descriptions still drive what gets planned; clicking a pin adds it to the day.
- **Trails and day trips.** Open-data hikes ingested from OpenStreetMap (43,400 routes across CH, AT, NO, FR), elevation-profiled against Copernicus GLO-30, quality-scored, and published only after a human approves each one in a dedicated review UI. The first 49 are live.
- **The practical layer.** Live water quality on swim coasts (EEA), crowding warnings at the extremes (JRC/Eurostat), 12-month climate comfort (WorldClim), per-country driving intel (tolls, vignettes), baggage policies per carrier, and deep country guides for all 43 countries.
- **Accounts and AI, optional.** Saved trips and plans behind Supabase auth with Google login. A Gemini-backed day planner and a booking-confirmation importer ("magic import") run as edge functions at zero standing cost. When the AI is off, the app says it is off.
- **Six languages.** English, Dutch, French, German, Spanish, Italian, across every surface including the KML, ICS and PDF exports.

## Where the numbers come from

The architecture is the one every serious price-discovery product uses: **precompute and cache, never live-search the map.** A background pipeline populates the data; the app serves static JSON from a CDN; the expensive lookup happens only when the traveller clicks through to book.

| Layer | Source | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Flight fares | Ryanair, Wizz Air, Vueling, Volotea, harvested per bookable day | 286 origins, ~900k day-prices, weekly rolling 150-day window |
| Fare backfill | Travelpayouts/Aviasales cache (carriers that cannot be scraped) | 66,781 dated quotes over 2,765 routes, 769 routes no direct harvest can see |
| Flight estimates | Quantile GBDT trained on the fare snapshot history, drift-gated retraining | route-month bands, shipped only with airline service evidence |
| Ground fares | One resolver: stored quote, then calibration artifact, then per-country priors | every leg priced, always flagged est or quote |
| Travel time | Transitous (MOTIS) door-to-door ground itineraries | per-origin reachability tables behind the hours filter |
| Coach network | Flix EU GTFS | 8,701 direct city pairs |
| Stays | Inside Airbnb city measurements + hostel and hotel tiers | review-based seasonality, length-of-stay discounts, fee modelling |
| Points of interest | OpenStreetMap, Overture, Wikidata, Wikipedia, Wikivoyage | 134,657 POIs, image and description enrichment sweeps |
| Environment | EEA bathing water, JRC/Eurostat crowding, WorldClim normals, GeoNames | per-destination badges and comfort curves |

All of it is orchestrated by one cadence-aware entry point (`run_pipeline.py`) that a Windows Scheduled Task fires weekly: fares refresh, carrier merges, backfill, estimate bands, snapshot archive, model retrain, freshness report, wire build. Popular origins can be refreshed on demand by staleness priority (`--max-origins N`). A 26-collector ingestion framework mirrors the raw open data (national timetable feeds, GTFS-RT, ADS-B, ferry schedules, pricing archives) with per-day manifests, and a separate PostGIS lab (with per-country Valhalla routing) does the heavy trail geometry work without ever touching the shipped data.

## Why the engineering holds up

- **Cheapest-wins with a truth hierarchy.** Four carrier harvests merge day by day; a cached quote wins a day only when it is strictly cheaper; an estimate never outranks any real fare regardless of price. Contract-A provenance (source, observed day, expiry, estimate flag) is stored in short keys so the wire stays slim.
- **Scale headroom.** The fare table is deduplicated per airport pair, the core wire is 5.2 MB for 1,570 destinations, per-origin fare slices load on demand, and continental-zoom map labels are grid-gated so symbol collision stays cheap at 25,000-destination scale.
- **Verification as a habit.** Twenty-plus headless harnesses (Playwright + node) assert the things that matter: every ground leg prices finitely with correct flags, every shipped estimate month is service-evidenced with zero violations, provenance chips render on every surface and never without data, the reach filter survives a URL round-trip and degrades silently without data, NaN can never become a price or a blank map.
- **Failure honesty.** Missing credentials skip cleanly, soft tasks never block the fare ship, the orchestrator backs up the master before every write and refuses concurrent writers, and anomalous fare files are dead-lettered by a schema gate before they can poison the model.
- **Licensing discipline.** Every external source is tracked in a license ledger ([docs/tos/data_licenses.md](tos/data_licenses.md)), the 24 sources that require visible credit are rendered in the app's footer, and caching-rights questions to commercial partners are drafted before any crawl is written.

## Running it

```
cd continent-app && npm install && npm run dev    # the app (sync-data runs first)
python run_pipeline.py --list                     # the pipeline, tasks + cadences
node scripts/verify_ground_fares.mjs              # example harness, from continent-app/
```
