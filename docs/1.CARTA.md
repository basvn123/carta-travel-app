# Carta

**Stop stitching trips together from five apps. Carta plans the whole trip in one place, with what each day costs.** Carta is one app for planning a trip anywhere in Europe, in place of the stack of planning tools. It prices every destination in its catalogue (the count is `meta.n_destinations` in `app_data.json`) across 43 countries: a bed plus food and drink, per person per day (local transport is not in the day figure yet). Then it plans the trip city by city and each day hour by hour, and hands each day and ride to the traveller's maps app ("Open in Google Maps"). Carta never navigates, and it does not price flights: the only flight figure in a total is one the traveller typed in.

Live at [carta-europetravel.com](https://carta-europetravel.com). The technical layout of this repository is in [README.md](../README.md); this document explains what has been built and why it can be trusted.

## The one rule everything follows

Carta's only real asset is trust in its numbers, so every price on every surface obeys the same chain:

**a measured figure first, then a cached third-party figure, then a model estimate, and never a blank.**

Each step is labelled for what it is. A measured figure shows its source and its age. A cached figure carries its expiry. An estimate is prefixed with a tilde, tagged "est.", and never dressed up as a bookable price. Every external booking link warns that prices may have changed. The pipeline enforces this end to end: provenance fields travel from the harvesters through the merge into the wire format and onto every pin, row, receipt line and detail sheet.

One consequence worth spelling out: Carta does not price flights (owner decision, 2026-10-02). No fare harvest has been live since 2026-10-01 and none is planned; the fare model and fare tables described in the pipeline are retired code. A traveller who has a fare types in what they paid, and only then does the trip total include the flight, as their own figure.

## What the traveller gets

- **The price map.** Every destination pinned with what a day there costs a person, for your lifestyle, rendered as WebGL layers that stay smooth at catalogue scale. The best value wears the only yellow flag on the map. Filters compose: price window, country, trip kinds, rating band, hidden gems, UNESCO, top beaches, and "reachable within 3/5/8/12 hours by train or bus" where travel-time data exists.
- **The receipt.** Carta's signature element. Every total is itemised line by line (stay, food and drink, and the traveller's own fare if they typed one in), each with its source, because the method belongs to the data and the receipt shows where each line came from.
- **The trip planner.** Multi-city routes with mode-aware legs: every train, bus, ferry and drive priced through one resolver, with sea crossings, tolls, vignettes, fuel from your own car model, and Omio or operator links per leg, each handing over to the maps app or the operator.
- **The day planner.** A map with the day drawn on it, and three tabs under it: today's plan (the places in order, and nothing else), add more (whole ready-made days, or a search and browse over the catalogue of 134,657 points of interest, 80,457 with photographs), and files (the traveller's own tickets and confirmations, kept on their device, plus a note for the group). Dwell times, walking budgets and must-see descriptions still drive what gets planned; clicking a pin adds it to the day.
- **Trails and day trips.** Open-data hikes ingested from OpenStreetMap (43,400 routes across CH, AT, NO, FR), elevation-profiled against Copernicus GLO-30, quality-scored, and published only after a human approves each one in a dedicated review UI. The first 49 are live.
- **The practical layer.** Live water quality on swim coasts (EEA), crowding warnings at the extremes (JRC/Eurostat), 12-month climate comfort (WorldClim), per-country driving intel (tolls, vignettes), and deep country guides for all 43 countries.
- **Accounts and AI, optional.** Saved trips and plans behind Supabase auth with Google login. A Gemini-backed day planner and a booking-confirmation importer ("magic import") run as edge functions at zero standing cost. When the AI is off, the app says it is off.
- **Six languages.** English, Dutch, French, German, Spanish, Italian, across every surface including the KML, ICS and PDF exports.

## Where the numbers come from

The architecture is the one every serious price-discovery product uses: **precompute and cache, never live-search the map.** A background pipeline populates the data; the app serves static JSON from a CDN; the expensive lookup happens only when the traveller clicks through to book.

| Layer | Source | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Ground fares | One resolver: stored quote, then calibration artifact, then per-country priors | every leg priced, always flagged est or quote |
| Travel time | Transitous (MOTIS) door-to-door ground itineraries | per-origin reachability tables behind the hours filter |
| Coach network | Flix EU GTFS | 8,701 direct city pairs |
| Stays | Inside Airbnb city measurements + hostel and hotel tiers | review-based seasonality, length-of-stay discounts, fee modelling |
| Points of interest | OpenStreetMap, Overture, Wikidata, Wikipedia, Wikivoyage | 134,657 POIs, image and description enrichment sweeps |
| Environment | EEA bathing water, JRC/Eurostat crowding, WorldClim normals, GeoNames | per-destination badges and comfort curves |

All of it is orchestrated by one cadence-aware entry point (`run_pipeline.py`) that a Windows Scheduled Task fires weekly: layer refreshes, merges, freshness report, wire build. Popular origins can be refreshed on demand by staleness priority (`--max-origins N`). A 26-collector ingestion framework mirrors the raw open data (national timetable feeds, GTFS-RT, ADS-B, ferry schedules, pricing archives) with per-day manifests, and a separate PostGIS lab (with per-country Valhalla routing) does the heavy trail geometry work without ever touching the shipped data.

## Why the engineering holds up

- **A truth hierarchy.** A measured figure outranks a cached one, and an estimate never outranks either. Contract-A provenance (source, observed day, expiry, estimate flag) is stored in short keys so the wire stays slim.
- **Scale headroom.** The core wire size is measured per build (the destination count is `meta.n_destinations` in `app_data.json`), and continental-zoom map labels are grid-gated so symbol collision stays cheap at 25,000-destination scale.
- **Verification as a habit.** Twenty-plus headless harnesses (Playwright + node) assert the things that matter: every ground leg prices finitely with correct flags, provenance chips render on every surface and never without data, the reach filter survives a URL round-trip and degrades silently without data, NaN can never become a price or a blank map.
- **Failure honesty.** Missing credentials skip cleanly, soft tasks never block the wire ship, the orchestrator backs up the master before every write and refuses concurrent writers, and anomalous data files are dead-lettered by a schema gate before they reach the wire.
- **Licensing discipline.** Every external source is tracked in a license ledger ([docs/tos/data_licenses.md](tos/data_licenses.md)), the sources that require visible credit are rendered in the Account panel's Data sources block (43 credit entries in `continent-app/src/data/attribution.js`, counted by its `source:` keys; European Environment Agency appears twice for two datasets, so 42 distinct organisations), and caching-rights questions to commercial partners are drafted before any crawl is written.

## Running it

```
cd continent-app && npm install && npm run dev    # the app (sync-data runs first)
python run_pipeline.py --list                     # the pipeline, tasks + cadences
node scripts/verify_ground_fares.mjs              # example harness, from continent-app/
```
