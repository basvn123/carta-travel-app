Carta is the one place to plan a trip in Europe and see what it really costs: the flight, the bed and every day there, in one total.
The problemPlanning a trip means five to eight apps, and none of them says what the days cost. A cheap flight can still lead to an expensive week.
The answerOne plan: find a place by what a day costs, price the whole trip, plan each day, share it, then hand over to Google Maps and the operators.
Why trust itNo booking and no commission, so no reason to push anything. Every estimate is marked as one; every flight price says where it came from.

The juggling Carta removes

A budget trip in Europe today, done the usual way. Each tool does one job and none of them talks to the others, so the traveller is the integration layer: copying dates, prices and places between tabs.

Your edges, exactly

Ranked by how hard they are to copy. "Core" edges need Carta's priced catalogue, so a competitor cannot add them with a feature sprint. "Strong" edges are real today and worth leading with. "Support" edges help a visitor decide, but do not carry a headline alone.

  1. All in one plan, less juggling Core

    "Stop stitching trips together from five apps."

    What it is
    The flight, the bed, the days, the route, the day plans, the group and the spend in one plan, where one change updates every figure.
    Proof
    Explore, Plan and My trips share one cost index (costIndex.js), so the card, the receipt, the planner and the day plan always show the same figure.
    Beats
    The eight-tool stack above. Wanderlog and Mindtrip come closest on planning, but they cannot price a place they were not told about.

  2. What a day costs, before you choose Core

    "A cheap flight is not a cheap trip."

    What it is
    A bed plus food and drink, per person per day, for every place in the catalogue, at the traveller's own travel style.
    Proof
    Priced from local rates where they were measured, with a tilde where a figure is a stand-in. About 3,900 places in 43 countries.
    Beats
    Every flight search (they stop at the airport) and every itinerary planner (they only know what you type).

  3. One honest total for the whole trip Core

    "The flight, the bed and every day there, in one total."

    What it is
    Google Flights' fare plus the beds plus the days, for the whole party, as one receipt with every line visible.
    Proof
    priceTrip() and PlanTotal.jsx; the flight carries its airline, times and check time.
    Beats
    Skyscanner and Google Flights, which price a leg, and Budget Your Trip, which prices an average day.

  4. Start from your budget, not a destination Strong

    "Tell Carta what you have. See where it takes you."

    What it is
    One field, "How much do you have?", and the places that fit, the ones that are tight, and what is left.
    Proof
    BudgetSearch.jsx with a 10 percent buffer line by default.
    Beats
    Kayak Explore and Skyscanner Everywhere filter by fare only. Nobody filters by the cost of the whole stay.

  5. Numbers you can trust Strong

    "Every estimate is marked as one."

    What it is
    A tilde on every stand-in figure, the flight named as Google Flights' with when it was checked, never a stored price shown as current.
    Proof
    formatEst(), the flight rules in PRODUCT.md, and the significance engine that rebuilt sight ratings from open evidence.
    Beats
    AI planners, whose reviews fault prices they cannot back and touristy picks.

  6. No booking, no commission, no push Strong

    "Carta sells nothing. It shows you the numbers and hands you to the operator."

    What it is
    Carta earns from the traveller (the pass), not from the hotel. It has no reason to rank a place higher because it pays more.
    Proof
    PRODUCT.md principle; affiliate links are a hand-off at the last step, never a ranking signal.
    Beats
    Booking.com AI, Expedia, Mindtrip and Layla, whose business is the booking.

  7. The walk and the town, priced together Strong

    "Komoot gets you round the loop. Carta tells you what the weekend costs."

    What it is
    Trails, beaches with EU bathing water quality, lakes, mountains and cycling routes, each beside the town you sleep in and what it costs.
    Proof
    About 17,600 trails and 22,300 bathing waters in the data layers; hikers are the launch audience.
    Beats
    Komoot, AllTrails and Outdooractive, which price nothing.

  8. Built for Europe, end to end Strong

    "Any trip in Europe, by plane, train, bus or car."

    What it is
    Small towns, low-cost airports, buses and trains, local prices, 43 countries, six European languages including the exports.
    Proof
    The catalogue, the six i18n files, the Getting there modes.
    Beats
    US-first planners (Wanderlog, Roadtrippers, TripIt, AllTrails) whose Europe coverage thins east of the Alps.

  9. Plan each day, then hand over to Google Maps Support

    "Plan every day. Open it in Google Maps."

    What it is
    Hour-by-hour or by parts of the day, with a day cost; then each day opens in the traveller's own maps app. No lock-in.
    Proof
    DayPlannerTab.jsx, the Google Maps hand-off rule, ICS and KML exports.
    Beats
    Google Maps itself, the best at getting there and the worst at planning.

  10. Plan together, settle up after Support

    "One link for the group. One place for the money."

    What it is
    Co-planners and a shared spend ledger inside the same trip.
    Proof
    ExpenseLedger.jsx, co-planners free since T488.
    Beats
    WhatsApp plus Tricount plus a shared doc. Lambus has this too, at a higher price.

  11. Bookings in, one itinerary out Support

    "Drop in your bookings. Carta fills the plan."

    What it is
    Booking confirmations become trip stops (Trip Pass).
    Proof
    MagicImportZone.jsx, the parse-booking edge function.
    Beats
    TripIt, which only starts once everything is booked.

  12. Ask in plain words, get real places with prices Support

    "Ask Carta. It answers with places you can afford."

    What it is
    A chat that answers from the priced catalogue, not from the open web.
    Proof
    browse/ask/, grounded on catalogue rows.
    Beats
    ChatGPT and Mindtrip on price truth; it does not try to beat them on chat breadth.

  13. A fair price, paid once Strong

    "Planning is free. A Trip Pass adds exports, more saved trips and more AI plans."

    What it is
    Free to plan; €6.99 for one trip (60 days) or €14.99 a year. No ads.
    Proof
    lib/pricing.js TIERS.
    Beats
    Stippl and Lambus (€24.99), Polarsteps Plus (€29.99), Wanderlog Pro (about $40), TripIt Pro ($48.99) and Komoot Premium (€59.99), all higher by the year (see the ladder below).

  14. A cost base that lets it stay cheap and independent Support

    Internal, not a headline.

    What it is
    Static precomputed data on a CDN: about €11 a month today, €55 to €75 at launch traffic, 88 to 93 percent gross margin.
    Proof
    CARTA_UNIT_ECONOMICS.md sections 2 and 5.
    Beats
    Venture-funded AI planners that must monetise each user through bookings.

Where they still win

Worth knowing before a visitor points it out. None of these is a reason to change the position; each is a reason not to claim it.

Do not compete here

  • Live fare depth. Skyscanner and Google Flights search every carrier live; Carta shows Google Flights' price from a sweep and one live search in the planner.
  • Navigation. Komoot and AllTrails guide you on the trail. Carta never navigates, by design.
  • Community size. Reviews, photos and user tours at Tripadvisor, AllTrails and Komoot scale. Carta has no testimonials and must not invent any.
  • Brand and native apps. Polarsteps and Wanderlog have store apps and large audiences. Carta is a web app with a store wrapper.
  • The world. Rome2Rio and Mindtrip cover the globe. Carta is Europe only.

Gaps to close in the product

  • Local transport and tickets are not in the day figure yet (roadmap 1.16, 2.14). Never say "everything included" until they are.
  • The airport-to-trail leg (T202-a): the claim no rival can copy, not built yet.
  • Proof of freshness is the only social proof Carta may use. Show when figures were checked.
  • Measurement: visitors, priced-trip completions and affiliate clicks have no instrument yet (LAUNCH-METRICS.md).

Business model

The model already in the repository, written out as a canvas. Carta earns from the traveller, not from the hotel, which is what lets it stay neutral.

Key partners

  • Google Flights (fare source, link out)
  • Omio, Trainline, Rome2Rio (ground legs, affiliate)
  • Booking and stay partners (hand-off)
  • Open data: Wikimedia, Wikidata, Wikivoyage, EEA, Eurostat, OSM, Overture
  • Gemini (Ask Carta, AI plans)
  • Stripe, Supabase, Cloudflare

Key activities

  • Harvest and price the catalogue (pipeline)
  • Keep figures fresh (weekly fares, monthly ratings)
  • Build planners and the day engine
  • Publish indexable pages for SEO

Key resources

  • The priced catalogue and its provenance chain
  • Trails, beaches, lakes, mountains layers
  • Six-language product
  • Trust in the numbers

Value proposition

  • See what the whole trip costs: flight, bed and days in one total
  • One place to plan: choose, price, route, days, share
  • Neutral: no booking, no commission
  • Honest: estimates marked, sources named
  • Hand-off to Google Maps and operators, no lock-in

Customer relationships

  • Self-serve, free to plan
  • Saved trips bring people back ("what changed since you saved")
  • Pay at the moment of value (export, more trips)

Channels

  • Organic search: one page per place, trail, beach, lake
  • Shared trip links and share cards
  • Store listings (wrapper)
  • No paid ads (unit economics rule)

Customer segments

  • Hikers first
  • Families looking for beaches
  • Cyclists and bikepackers
  • Budget city breaks
  • Car-free travellers
  • Students, young workers, retirees
  • Europe-based, planning on a phone

Cost structure

  • Infrastructure about €11 a month today, €55 to €75 at launch traffic
  • AI per use: about €0.01 a plan, €0.05 a grounded search, capped per pass
  • Stripe €0.25 plus about 1.5 percent; 21 percent VAT inside the price
  • Owner time (the main cost)

Revenue streams

  • Trip Pass €6.99 once, 60 days: every PDF, ICS and KML export, more saved trips, booking import, 60 AI plans
  • Yearly plan €14.99, renews: the same for a year, 300 AI plans; pays off from the third trip
  • Affiliate hand-offs on ground legs and stays: about €0.01 per monthly user, linear with traffic
  • Later, if wanted: partner data licences or a white-label cost widget for tourist boards (not in the repo, an idea to test)

Growth engine

  • Every place, trail, beach and lake is an indexable page with a figure nobody else publishes: "what a day in Bovec costs".
  • Every shared trip is an invitation: the link shows the total and the route.
  • Saved trips bring travellers back when prices move.
  • The home page converts the visitor who arrives from search: it must make the value clear in one screen.

Risks, in order

  • Conversion under 1 percent (travel converts low): run the price test, instrument the paywall before launch.
  • AI price rises: caps hold; per-fact grounding removes most of the exposure.
  • App store fees of 15 to 30 percent if passes are sold in the wrapper.
  • A rival adds day costs: the moat is the catalogue and its freshness, so keep the pipeline running.

What to do with this

  • Lead every surface with edges 1 to 3

    All in one plan, what a day costs, one honest total. They are the reason to switch. Edges 4 to 8 are the proof row under the hero.

  • Use edges 5 and 6 as the trust line

    "No booking, no commission. Every estimate marked." It answers the question every AI planner raises.

  • Speak to one traveller at a time

    Hiker first, then families at the beach, then budget city breaks. Each gets one example with a real place and its day price.

  • Build the airport-to-trail leg

    The one claim no rival can copy (T202-a). It joins the departure airport to the outdoor layers.

  • Turn this into the home page

    Part two, the homepage playbook, gives the sentences and the section-by-section build.
