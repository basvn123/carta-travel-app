The message, before any design

A visitor gives the first screen about five seconds. The competitor analysis gives three edges no rival holds together. Every section of the page repeats one of them; nothing on the page introduces a fourth.

The promisePlan the whole trip in one place, and see what it costs before you book.
Edge 1, all in one

One plan, not five apps

Choose, price, route, plan each day and share, without copying between tabs.

Edge 2, the days

What a day costs, everywhere

A bed plus food and drink, per person, in every place, before you choose it.

Edge 3, the total

One honest total

Google Flights' fare, the beds and the days, for your whole party.

The trust line under all three: No booking, no commission. Every estimate is marked as one.
The clarity testShow the hero to someone for five seconds, then ask: "What does this app do?" The answer must contain "plans a trip" and "what it costs". Wise's 2026 teardown is the warning: its real advantage (fair rate, visible fee) sat below the fold. Carta puts "what it costs" in the headline.

Six hero options

Each pairs a headline (a verb or a figure, under eight words) with one subline that completes it. The recommended one names both halves of the promise, so the page never depends on the subline to be understood.

Recommended
Plan the whole trip. See what it costs.

The flight, the bed and every day there, in one plan and one total. For any trip in Europe.

Two verbs, two edges. A stranger knows it plans and it prices.

Keep as section title
A cheap flight is not a cheap trip.

Carta puts the flight, the bed and every day there in one plan, with one honest total.

Today's slogan. The best line for the problem, weaker as a first line: it says what is wrong, not what Carta is. Move it to the week card.

All in one led
Five apps for one trip? Use one.

Carta plans the route, the stays and every day, and tells you what each day costs.

Names the juggling directly, Splitwise style: the feeling it removes.

Cost led
Know what a trip costs before you go.

See the bed and the days in every place in Europe, then plan the whole trip in one place.

Clearest for the budget audience; plans comes second.

Budget first
How far does your budget go?

Tell Carta what you have. See the places that fit, then plan the trip and every day.

Pairs with the budget field as the hero's input. A question is allowed; it carries a verb.

Short slogan
Your trip. One plan. One total.

The flight, the bed and every day there, planned and priced in one place.

The Mindtrip manner: very short, set huge. Fails the verb rule on its own, so only with a verb in the sub.

Take the scale and the slow film. Add what only Carta can say: "what it costs". Short, but not generic.

One app for all your travel planning needs
Wanderlog, homepage tagline, words only
WanderlogAll in one

The direct rival's all-in-one claim. It promises organising, not knowing: Wanderlog prices only what you type in.

One plan instead of five apps.
And the only one that knows what each day costs.

Do claim all in one, then win on the half Wanderlog cannot say. Never "all your needs": Carta does not book or navigate.

MindtripStory panels

Each chapter is one rounded colour panel with tabs, a looping video and chips that drift around it.

Find it. Price it. Plan it.
Five panels, each playing a recording of the real app: Find, Price, Plan, Your days, Share.

Chips carry real figures: "Bovec ~€89 a day", "Bus to Bled €7". A Mindtrip chip says a mood; a Carta chip says a price.

HeadspaceThe need

"Be kind to your mind". The page shows a person stating the need in their own words, and the product answering.

Ask it like you would ask a friend.
"A beach for two kids in June, under €90 a day." Carta answers with a real place and its day price.

Six tabs, one per traveller: hikers, families at the beach, cyclists, without a car, city breaks, tight budget.

KomootMeaning

The line ends in a price. That is the one thing a Komoot drawing can never say.

Plain columns, hairlines, a tick list each, one button under the recommended plan.

Free, Trip Pass €6.99 once, Yearly plan €14.99.

HopperClose

A clean footer with the logo on top and link columns. Hopper's own line: "Spend Less, Travel Better".

Where do you want to go next?
The Ask Carta field one last time, then the footer: Explore, Plan, Carta, Legal.

End on a question with the field under it, so the last screen is an action, not a goodbye.

LinearProduct in use

"The product development system for teams and agents". The product UI is the hero image. Live animation of the interface can feel slow on weaker machines.

This is Carta, recorded.
Real screen recordings in a phone frame, played only in view. Every tap in them happened in the app.

Show, then say. A visitor understands "one plan" faster from ten seconds of the planner than from any sentence.

The copy bank

Every line is checked against Carta's rules: a verb or a figure in each headline, no banned words, no dashes, no counts of places, never "free" without the pass sentence, never "navigate". Press Copy to take one.
