# The Significance Engine

**How Carta decides what is actually worth seeing.** In August 2026 the catalogue's point-of-interest ratings were rebuilt from the ground up: the harvest-time importance scores that came bundled with the data were replaced by a composite significance score computed from independent open signals, applied only through a validation gate, and recalibrated town by town. This document explains what was wrong, what was built, and why the new ratings can be trusted. The product overview is in [1.CARTA.md](1.CARTA.md); the working plan and audit trail live in [docs/QUALITY_UPGRADE_PLAN.md](docs/QUALITY_UPGRADE_PLAN.md).

## The problem, measured

Every POI in the catalogue carries a `rate` from 0 to 3, and the whole app leans on it: the day planner's must-see shelf, the deck ranking, the PDF exports, even each destination's own 0-10 rating (its "things to do" component saturates on rate-weighted counts). Until this work, that number was whatever the harvesters said: OpenTripMap's generous 0-3, Overture imports capped at 2, a sitelink backfill for thin towns. Nobody had ever checked it at catalogue scale.

A new audit pass ([pipeline/audit_quality.py](pipeline/audit_quality.py)) did, and the findings were bad:

- **29.8% of all 134,657 POIs were rated "top sight."** A tier that includes a third of everything distinguishes nothing.
- **192 destinations had over 45% of their entire list at the top tier** (one national park sat at 90%). Nature harvests were the worst offenders: every peak, every parking-lot trailhead, a must-see.
- **1,523 duplicate pairs across 792 destinations**: Park Güell twice under two spellings, castles under three languages.
- **3,465 top-rated POIs had no image**, because "top-rated" and "notable enough for anyone to have photographed" had nothing to do with each other.
- **The fame signal was dead in production.** Wikipedia pageviews had been harvested for 17k POIs and were read by the front-end's ranking function, but the apply step had never run: not one POI in the shipped data carried the field.
- The absurd concrete cases: "Spanair Flight 5022" (a plane crash article geocoded in Madrid), "Wrestling at the 1992 Summer Olympics," and defunct stadiums all wore the same top badge as the Sagrada Família.

Coordinates, for the record, were clean: zero null-island points, zero POIs outside the Europe bounding box.

## The one rule the new ratings follow

Mirroring the pricing chain in [1.CARTA.md](1.CARTA.md), significance now has its own truth hierarchy:

**independent open evidence first, editorial judgement weighted in, harvest opinion demoted to a prior, and no top rating without corroboration.**

A POI cannot hold the top tier on a harvester's say-so alone. It needs at least one independent witness: a Wikipedia article of its own, a formal heritage designation, or a human Wikivoyage editor having listed it.

## The composite score

[pipeline/score_significance.py](pipeline/score_significance.py) scores every live POI from five signals:

| Signal | Weight | Source | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pageviews | 0.30 | Wikimedia REST API, 12-month daily average | Attention: how many people actually look this up |
| Sitelinks | 0.30 | Wikidata, count of language editions | Language-neutral notability: how many Wikipedias bothered |
| Heritage | 0.15 | Wikidata P1435 + the OTM register flag, boosted by P1174 visitor counts where present | Expert designation, independent of popularity |
| Wikivoyage listing | 0.15 | See/Do listing membership, weighted by how early the editor placed it | Human curation: someone decided a visitor should know |
| Old rate | 0.10 | The previous harvest-time rate | A weak prior, so uncorroborated but plausibly real places do not collapse to zero |

Pageviews and sitelinks are log-transformed then z-scored (both are heavy-tailed; Paris must not define the scale). The blended score is then converted to a percentile two ways and mixed **60% within the destination, 40% across the whole catalogue**. That per-town normalisation is the load-bearing trick from the underlying research playbook: the best sight of a small Estonian island keeps its local top billing even though Rome would crush it in a global ranking.

New rates are assigned by local quota: roughly the **top 12% of a town's list earns rate 3** (minimum one for any town with six or more POIs, hard cap 12), the **next 28% earns rate 2**, the rest rate 1. Corroboration is required for the top tier. Two guard rails sit on top:

- **A heritage floor.** A formally designated monument with 15+ sitelinks can never fall below rate 2, whatever the quota says. This exists because in POI-dense Brussels a UNESCO ensemble genuinely ranked 60th and would otherwise have been buried by the quota.
- **Noise stays demoted.** Commercial-noise POIs (rate 0) and duplicates are excluded from scoring and quotas entirely.

## The signal harvests

Three new or completed harvesters feed the scorer, all cache-only, resumable, and politely throttled with a contact User-Agent:

- **[pipeline/harvest_poi_wikidata.py](pipeline/harvest_poi_wikidata.py)** resolves all ~26,000 wiki-linked POIs to their Wikidata QID (batched pageprops, 50 titles per request, redirects followed), then pulls sitelink counts, heritage designation, and annual visitor counts via batched SPARQL (200 QIDs per query, serial, inside WDQS's published limits). Result: 25,372 POIs with sitelinks, 9,439 with formal heritage designations, 699 with real visitor counts.
- **[pipeline/harvest_wikivoyage_listings.py](pipeline/harvest_wikivoyage_listings.py)** parses every destination's Wikivoyage article wikitext for `{{see}}` and `{{do}}` listing templates (name, alternate name, coordinates, QID, and the editor's ordering) plus the article's review status. Result: 13,248 listings across 1,010 articles, including 4 Star and 103 Guide articles. Listings match POIs by QID first, then by folded name core plus proximity. Only facts are stored (names, coordinates, order), never prose.
- **The POI pageview harvest was finished**: 34,464 articles now have a 12-month daily average, zero failures, and the long-dead apply step finally ran, so 22,386 POIs ship a real `pop` value the day planner's ranking has been silently wishing for since July.

### The misattribution trap, and why entity typing matters

The first full scoring run produced a Brussels top list led by "Manneken Peace" (a knockoff statue) above Manneken Pis itself, with the Schaerbeek district and the central railway station in the top ten, while Grand Place fell to tier 2. The cause: **6,568 POIs' wiki links resolve to their town's or district's article, and 252 to station articles.** A statue whose link points at the Brussels city article inherits Brussels' 132 sitelinks and 1,900 daily views.

The fix is entity-type awareness. The Wikidata harvester also fetches instance-of class flags through the subclass tree (administrative territorial entity, human settlement, railway station), and for any POI whose article is really a place-of-residence or a station, the scorer zeroes the attention signals, refuses wiki-based corroboration, and the apply step strips its poisoned `pop`. After that one change, Brussels reads exactly as it should: Manneken Pis, Grand Place, Atomium, cathedral, Town Hall.

## The validation gate

Following the playbook's benchmark discipline, the apply step **refuses to write the master** unless all of these hold (checked on every run, logged to `logs/significance_report.json`):

- Every famous-heritage anchor (formally designated, 25+ sitelinks) lands tier 2 or higher. Final run: **1,392 of 1,392, recall 1.0**.
- Wikivoyage editors' top picks (the first third of See listings) land tier 2 or higher, recall at least 0.80. Final run: **0.957 across 2,347 picks**.
- **Zero uncorroborated POIs hold the top tier.**

The final applied run rewrote 63,674 rates. Rank order within towns was preserved where it deserved to be (median per-destination Spearman correlation 0.75 against the old rates); the movers ledger records every change of two tiers or more for review.

## Data hygiene shipped alongside

- **Index-stable dedupe** ([pipeline/dedupe_pois.py](pipeline/dedupe_pois.py)). Saved day plans reference POIs by position, so the master pass never deletes or reorders: 7,202 near-duplicates are tagged `dup: true` in place and 507 signal fields (images, wiki links, descriptions) merged into the surviving twin. The identity rules port the front-end's union-find but are deliberately stricter, because a false merge in the master is permanent: image and geo-cell matches require a shared distinctive name token (document-frequency filtered so a city's own name is not evidence), and names carrying different explicit years never merge (the two battles of Breitenfeld stay two battles).
- **Commercial noise demoted.** The kind-normalisation pass existed but had never been applied to the current master: 1,957 bars, aparthotels and car parks filed as sights now carry rate 0 and a noise marker.
- **Destination ratings recalibrated.** Each destination's 0-10 rating keeps its 70% hand-curated appeal component untouched, but the "depth of things to do" component now ignores duplicates and noise, and its saturation constant was re-derived (28 to 19) so the deflated rate distribution keeps the same calibrated spread. Tier counts held steady (40 / 150 / 413) through the layer's own validation gate.
- **Two real bugs fixed.** The rating shadow report (the human review queue comparing curators against data) read a field that only exists in the slimmed wire format, so its Wikivoyage component had been silently zero for every destination, biasing the whole queue toward "curators too generous." It now reads the master's field and also uses Wikivoyage's own review ladder (star/guide/usable) as a quality signal. And the master writers that still wrote non-atomically now go through the crash-safe temp-file-and-rename helper.
- **A freedom-of-panorama review queue.** France, Italy, Belgium, Greece and ten other covered countries have no freedom of panorama: photographs of modern buildings and public art there are copyright-encumbered even on Wikimedia Commons. The audit now flags images on modern-work kinds in those countries (8,542 currently) into a standing review queue. Report-only; the policy decision is a human one.

## Results, before and after

| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| POIs rated "top sight" | 29.8% | 9.9% |
| Destinations with an inflated top tier (>45% rate-3) | 192 | 0 |
| Top-rated POIs without an image | 3,465 | 408 |
| POIs shipping a real pageview signal | 0 | 22,386 |
| POIs with Wikidata identity (QID + sitelinks) | 0 | 25,372 |
| POIs with a formal heritage designation signal | 0 (register flag only) | 9,439 |
| Duplicate POIs tagged and signal-merged | 0 | 7,202 |
| Commercial noise demoted | 0 | 1,957 |

The 408 remaining imageless top sights are a data-availability floor, not a harvest gap: 404 of them are cached misses from the full Commons geosearch sweep, meaning no freely licensed geotagged photo exists near them at all.

## Automation and provenance

Two tasks joined the weekly orchestrator's registry:

- **`poi_significance` (monthly):** pageviews, Wikidata signals, Wikivoyage listings, dedupe, noise normalisation, the gated re-tier, then the destination rating refresh, in that order. Every harvest is resumable, every write atomic, and the gate can abort the apply without harming anything upstream.
- **`audit` (monthly, report-only):** the full quality scorecard, so drift in any of these numbers is seen rather than suspected.

Licensing stays disciplined: Wikidata is CC0, the Wikivoyage listing signal is derived from facts rather than prose (the CC BY-SA blurbs were already credited in the footer), and both new call patterns are recorded in the [license ledger](docs/tos/data_licenses.md). Schema changes (`rate` semantics, `dup`, `noise`, `pop`) are documented in [SCHEMA.md](SCHEMA.md), which also had its stale rating-v1 section corrected to the shipped v2.

## Running it

```
python pipeline/audit_quality.py                    # the scorecard, read-only
python pipeline/score_significance.py               # score + validate, no writes
python pipeline/score_significance.py apply         # gated apply to the master
python run_pipeline.py --only poi_significance      # the whole monthly chain
```

After any re-harvest, the sanity ritual is: read the gate block in `logs/significance_report.json`, then eyeball one dense city's top twelve (Brussels should open with Manneken Pis and Grand Place, and no district, station or knockoff should appear).

## Honest limits

- Big-city Wikivoyage articles delegate their See/Do listings to district sub-articles, which are not followed; the listing signal is strongest exactly where it matters most, in small towns with a single article.
- 514 residual fuzzy duplicate pairs were deliberately left unmerged (different kind families, translated names without shared tokens); the front-end's runtime suppression continues to hide them, and merging them at the master would risk false welds.
- Popularity signals encode bias toward famous, photographed, English-Wikipedia-covered places. The per-town normalisation, the heritage floor and the Wikivoyage weight push against it, but do not eliminate it. The curated appeal layer, which outweighs all of this at the destination level, remains hand-scored on purpose.
