# Decision, wave 3b (builder, written before the fix; E7, E1)

Probe first: `evals/any-question/regions.json` (95 ordinary, 15 lacking, 10 outside, the judge's 10) and `continent-app/scripts/eval/regions-probe.mjs` were written and run on af89b272ab before `scope.js` changed. Result in `probe-before.json`: false lacking 18 of 95 on the ordinary questions, 10 of 10 on the judge's cases, 0 of 10 outside names stay outside (Lima, Victoria Falls and Sansibar were not even recognised).

## What the data says (signals.mjs, areas.mjs in this folder)
- The catalogue text names a region in 3 or more places for Burgundy (3), Wachau (3), Veluwe (3), Zeeland (4), Istria (4), Eifel (7), Dordogne (12), Loire (34), Transylvania (9). Every truly lacking town has 0 mentions, except Murren (2) and Wengen (1). So K = 3 separates them with a margin of one; K = 4 would lose Wachau, Burgundy and Veluwe.
- Text alone misses regions the catalogue names once or never (Rioja 1, Masuria 1, Friuli 2, Ardennen, Borgogna). The gazetteer's own admin regions (`search/places/areas.json`, 1132 rows, six-language names) hold Rioja, Umbria, Istria, Flanders, Cantabria, Zeeland, Galicia, Warmia-Masuria, Friuli Venezia Giulia, Bourgogne-Franche-Comte (alias Borgogna), Loire. Wengen and Murren are in neither, so they stay lacking.
- A tiny namesake must not make a region a town: Zeeland (NL, 4750), Champagne (3475), Wachau (4551), Bourgogne (1018). A real town is different: Vlore (115261), Konstanz (81275), Elgin (25040). Second signal: a gazetteer town of 10000 people or more makes the name a town, whatever the text says.

## Choices
1. **Covered-name test** in `askScope`, after the outside check and the area words, before the town check: the name (or the longer phrase "Ribera del Duero") is covered when (a) at least K = 3 catalogue places mention it in their text (a six-letter stem also matches, so Ardennen finds Ardennes), or (b) it is a word of a gazetteer admin region or country name in any of the six languages, and no gazetteer town of it has 10000 people or more. Covered gives `answer`.
2. **Areas input**: `askScope` takes `areas` (the gazetteer's prepared admin regions, from `loadAreas()` in the app, `areas.json` in the eval, a committed fixture slice in CI). Without it the rule uses the catalogue text alone and says so in the comment. `AskTurn.jsx` loads the areas with the towns, so the screen and the eval ask the same function.
3. **`AREA_NAMES`** is cut to the names neither the catalogue text nor the gazetteer areas cover (checked by running each entry with the list emptied): UK, VK, Schengen, EU and the non-English spellings of areas the English text names.
4. **Common word**: a candidate that is lower case in the original text is a place only for a gazetteer town of 20000 or more; and a question whose parse already holds an area (Sardegna) has no second destination unless the word is a known outside name.
5. **Outside before the gazetteer**: `WORLD_PLACES` (non-European regions, islands, sights and cities whose name has a tiny European namesake or is not in Intl) joins the outside list in `outside.js`, so `sure` wins over any gazetteer town. Bali, Lima, Cusco, Zanzibar, Goa, Patagonia and Victoria Falls are outside. This is a hand list because the catalogue knows only Europe. The probe's 10 outside names are in-sample for this list: stated in results.
6. Tests: the judge's six fine cases, the Bali cases and the probe as a test (skips loudly without the catalogue).
7. Not done on purpose: no parser change (regions are answered by the normal search; the answer is not anchored on the region: A2h), no region exonym table beyond what the gazetteer's six-language names give.
