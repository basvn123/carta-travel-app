## T608: the page

### What changed
The home page is new from the first line. Top to bottom:

The hero is a film on a rounded stage: five travellers' places one after another (Bovec, Albufeira, Český Krumlov, Porto, Ohrid), each a licensed Commons photograph pushed in slowly until the licensed clips of row T605-b arrive. On every shot a solid pill names the place and its own day price, and a second gives the cheapest return flight there from the traveller's home airport, Google Flights' price from the sweep ("Porto €98 a day", "Return flight €133"): the cheap part beside what a day costs, which is the page's whole point, on its first screen. The pills drift on 8 and 9 s loops; a pause control stops the film. Under the stage, on paper: the slogan, one line of what Carta does, Ask Carta's field (the question goes to Explore), "Plan a trip" as the page's one primary and "Log in" beside it (D7). On a phone the brand and Log in sit on a top line of their own and the whole hero, primary included, fits the first screen.

Then the point in figures, the owner's Wise reference: a card with five places and 3, 5 or 7 nights, the trip's total a person in large tabular figures, and its lines (the return flight with the airline and when Google Flights was checked, the bed, food and drink). The headline says the two figures side by side. Without a flight from home the card says so and the total is the ground alone.

Then the six travellers as tabs (the Headspace reference): each asks in its own words, the question types itself out, Carta answers with one real place, its photograph, its day price and the door to that search. Then the story as five coloured panels (the Mindtrip reference), each playing a recording of the app doing that step (T609). Then the story's trip drawn on Slovenia (the Komoot reference): the line draws itself, the towns arrive with their nights, the total a person last. Then five promises as an Apple-style rail, the three plans as Linear-style columns with the free sentence, the question again, and the site footer (T594).

Every figure is the catalogue's or Google Flights': no count, no method sentence, no testimonial. The motion is CSS keyframes and transitions on transform and opacity plus `stroke-dashoffset` and one `clip-path` wipe, as the six references do it (T605): no animation library. Everything moves only on screen, in a visible tab, and nothing under reduced motion.
