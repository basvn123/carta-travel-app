# Carta rating v5: a rating that makes sense, and a match that fits you

Oct 27, 2026 · @Bas

## In short

Carta's rating becomes three separate answers: a **quality score with five bands** every place earns on one written rubric, **the same scale for nature items** with labels for POIs, and a **personal match** from a four-question taste profile that reorders lists without ever changing a place's score.

- **Quality.** One formula for all 3,868 destinations, judged inside five size classes (metro, city, town, village, area) so a village can reach the top band. Every place gets a rubric score and a reason sentence, scored by Claude in Claude Code sessions. Calibration is frozen, so a new place moves nothing else.
- **Nature and POIs.** Trails, beaches, lakes, mountains and cycling share one scale set Europe-wide within each kind, with a regional rank and facts (difficulty, water class) beside it. POIs get Must-see, Recommended or Worth a look, capped per place.
- **For you.** Who comes, what you go for (the planner's 12 likes), city or village and lively or quiet, and your Lifestyle. Asked once in Explore's start dialog and kept in Account. The same `forYou()` ranks Explore, "Help me choose", the stay step and Ask Carta.
- **What a traveller sees.** "8.9 Exceptional, top 2% of villages in Europe" and "86% match", with a bar per like and the fact behind it on the place page (decided 27 October).

It replaces roadmap 2.10 and the rating half of 2.7, and gives 2.13 (the month score) a reason for every place to build on. All nine owner decisions were taken on 27 October.

## How rating works today

Carta has seven rating scales, each with its own cutoffs and words, and only the destination one has a written method; that, not any single bug, is why a score is hard to explain.

| What | Model (file) | Scale and tiers | How many | Main weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Destinations | rating\_v4 (`pipeline/rating_layer.py`) | 0 to 10; 8.7 / 7.8 / 6.9 = journey / detour / visit | 3,868 (tiers 3/2/1/none: 48 / 218 / 1,213 / 2,389) | Two formulas: 1,570 hand-scored, 2,288 fitted by a regression refit every run |
| Trails | open-signals-v2 (`pipeline/trails/rate.py`) | 4.0 to 9.8 stretched per country; app reuses 8.7 / 7.8 / 6.9 | 17,619 | Min-max per country: the best trail in Belgium scores like the best in Switzerland |
| Beaches | beach\_beauty\_v2 (`pipeline/beaches/beauty_index.py`) | 8.6 / 7.6 / 6.4 | 2,745 rated, 3,873 listed | Missing parts dropped and weights renormalised, so thin data can score high |

### The destination rating in one paragraph

Hand-scored places get 0.70 appeal (from `app_data/curated_appeal.json`, scaled per class) + 0.13 beauty + 0.06 acclaim (UNESCO, beautiful villages, parks) + 0.11 highlights (best six POIs). The rest get a least-squares fit on acclaim, beauty and highlights, mapped through frozen curves per class and capped near 8.5. Classes come from population (`pipeline/place_layer.py:140-170`): metro 127, city 614, town 996, village 1,675, area 456. Gates and two test suites guard the result (`tests/test_rating_distribution.py`, `tests/test_golden_ratings.py` with 120 pairs).

## Market research

The guides travellers trust most use three named tiers with a written method, not a decimal score; personal fit is shown as a separate signal with a reason, and taste is asked in three to five questions.

| Who | What they show | Levels | What Carta takes from it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

What the research says about people:

- **Levels.** People reliably tell apart about six levels of one thing ([Miller](https://www.yorku.ca/pclassic/Miller/)); three tiers plus "not rated" sit well inside that.
- **Averages mislead.** Review scores are J-shaped, so the mean is a poor proxy for quality ([Hu, Pavlou, Zhang](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/780)); a score without its evidence is distrusted, and 4.5 from 57 beats 5.0 from 4 ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/user-perception-of-product-ratings)).
- **Explain the match.** Name the data behind a suggestion ("Because you picked quiet coast"), group suggestions, let people edit their inputs and see results change at once ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recommendation-guidelines/), [NN/g calculators](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recommendations-calculator/)).
- **Short quizzes.** Onboarding quizzes average 3.9 question screens; every question must change the result ([Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/how-many-quiz-questions-onboarding)).
- **Choice overload** is real when preferences are uncertain and the task is hard ([Chernev et al.](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual)), which is exactly the "where should I go" moment.
- **Personas predict poorly.** Plog's traveller types are widely cited but weakly predictive; treat taste as weights, not an identity.

Lessons for Carta:

1. Most trusted guides use three tiers, but people can tell about six levels apart. Carta uses five bands with the score visible (owner decision, 27 October): Exceptional, Excellent, Very good, Good, Pleasant, then no badge.
2. Pair the number with its evidence and a rank inside the class ("Top 2% of villages in Europe"), so a number never stands alone.
3. Rank within a class, then one shared scale, so a village can reach the top band.
4. Always show the evidence: the reason, measured or estimated, and what fed it.
5. Quality and personal match are two signals, shown side by side and never merged. The match shows its data: a bar per like with the fact behind it, as NN/g advises.
6. Four onboarding questions, each skippable, each one changing the ranking; then refine from what people save.
7. Outdoor items get the same scale plus facts with stated limits (difficulty, length, climb, water quality), and certifications stay badges.
8. Re-check scores on a schedule and allow demotion, so the top band stays believable.

## Design principles

Bulletproof means three things: a score never moves when something else is added, every score says why and how sure it is, and a village is judged as fairly as a capital.

1. **Three questions, three answers.** "Is it good?" (quality, the same for everyone), "Is it good in that month?" (roadmap 2.13) and "Is it good for me?" (the match). They are never mixed silently into one number.
2. **Stable by construction.** Every scale is calibrated once on a frozen reference set and versioned. Adding 100 places moves no existing score by more than 0.05; adding a lake moves no other lake's tier.
3. **Every score carries its reason and its confidence.** One sentence a traveller could repeat to a friend, make sure there is an explanation for each score
4. **Fair across sizes.** A village is compared with villages, a city with cities, then placed on one shared tier ladder so a great village and a great city can both earn the top tier.
5. **The same rubric in every session.** A written rubric with anchor places per class, so a destination added in a Claude Code session six months from now is scored the way Rome and Charleroi were.
6. **Quality, not fame.** Popularity stays out of the score (owner decision, kept). Fame only appears as a fact ("busy in August").
7. **Personal never hides.** The match reorders and highlights; it never drops a place silently, and the base tier is always one tap away.
8. **No runtime AI and no Claude API.** The match is a small formula that runs in the browser on data already in the wire.
9. **Thin data says so.** Missing facts show as "Not yet" (the NotYet slot), never a guessed number, and an estimated score cannot reach the top tier.

## Layer 1: the place quality score

Every destination is scored with one written rubric inside its own size class, on one formula, with frozen calibration; the traveller sees the score, one of five band words, the rank inside its class and a reason.

### Five classes, judged as what they are

| Class | Rule (from `place_layer.py`, kept) | Today | What "worth the trip" means for it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Metro | 300,000 or more people | 127 | A capital-class city you can fill a week in |
| City | 50,000 or more | 614 | Two or three full days, a centre you remember |
| Town | 8,000 or more | 996 | A full day, a reason to stay one night |
| Village | under 8,000 | 1,675 | A place people detour for, like Conques or Hallstatt |
| Area (countryside, island, region) | no centre, or nature-tagged under 1,200 | 456 | A landscape you base yourself in, like a valley or an island |

Each class has its own rubric anchors and its own frozen curve with a ceiling (metro 10, the others 9.6 to 9.7, as now), so a village can reach the top tier on village terms. A sanity gate keeps every class between 0.5% and 4% in the top tier.

### One formula for every place

```latex
Q = 0.60\,A + 0.15\,B + 0.15\,D + 0.10\,R
```

| Part | What it measures | Change from v4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, appeal | The rubric score, 0 to 10, with its one-sentence reason | Every place gets one, not 1,570 of 3,868; no second fitted formula |
| B, beauty | `beauty_layer.py` urban fabric, natural setting, water and iconic views within 5 km | UNESCO, fame and village labels leave it; beaches measured per place, not per country |
| D, depth | How many good things there are to do, across distinct kinds, against the class median | Replaces "best six POIs", which saturates at 0.98 to 1.0 |
| R, recognition | Best register, then a smaller step for each extra one | Geopark 0.30 and biosphere reserve 0.25 get their own lower weights instead of counting as a national park (0.62) |

The weights are a starting point. They are tuned once on the 120 golden pairs plus the new persona set, then frozen with the version.

### Each part in depth

Each part answers one question, counts each fact once, and writes its own one-line explanation, so the breakdown on the place page explains every number in it.

#### A, appeal (60%): how good is this place as a whole?

The only part that judges the whole place; the other three are measured checks that keep it honest.

- **Five criteria**, each judged from the fact card:
  - Setting: the first impression and where it sits (hilltop, lakeshore, coast, valley).
  - Character: an intact old core, authenticity, how much traffic and sprawl.
  - Signature sights: what a visitor will still remember a year later.
  - Atmosphere: life on the streets and in the evening for a city; calm and care for a village.
  - Uniqueness: is there anything like it within 100 km, or in Europe?
- **Score** = the band whose test fits, then the position inside the band against four anchor places per class and band (chosen in T757, approved by you).
- **Explanation:** the reason sentence names the strongest criterion and one limit: "Walled hilltop village above the Alqueva lake; little to do after half a day."
- **Guards:** Claude scores before seeing the v4 score; a gap of more than 1.5 with the v4 score or the estimate gets a second look; you check a random 5% per batch; the golden pairs must still hold.

#### B, beauty (15%): how does it look?

Today `beauty_layer.py` blends heritage 0.27 (UNESCO sites within 60 km), nature 0.21 (scenic tags), iconic 0.24 (curated list, fame and beautiful-village membership), beach 0.08 (the country's Blue Flag count) and urban 0.20 (walkable core, heritage density, squares, canals, bridges, walls), plus a standout bonus of 0.20.

| Part | v5 | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Urban fabric | 0.35, unchanged method | Already measured per place from OSM |
| Natural setting | 0.35: scenic tags plus rated lakes, peaks and viewpoints within 5 km | Tags alone are sparse; layer 2 has the real items |
| Water | 0.20: the place's own rated beaches and lakes within 5 km | Replaces the country Blue Flag count, which gives every Spanish coastal town the same value |
| Iconic view | 0.10: the curated list of famous views only | Fame goes (quality, not fame) and beautiful-village membership moves to R |
| UNESCO | removed | Counted in R; today it is counted twice |

The standout bonus stays, so a single-dimension stunner such as a lone fjord village still scores high. **Explanation** from the two strongest parts: "Beauty 8.2: walled core with three squares, a lake below."

#### D, depth (15%): how much is there to do?

It replaces "highlights" (0.55 × best POI + 0.45 × the top six), which reaches 0.98 to 1.0 for almost every famous place and rewards one big sight over variety.

- **Counted:** POIs labelled Must-see or Recommended (after the re-quota of layer 2), plus nature items rated Very good or better within 30 minutes, sorted into the eight categories (sights, museums, nature, food and drink, active, family, nightlife, markets).
- **Diminishing returns:** within a category the first item counts 1, the second 1/2, the third 1/3, up to four; a small bonus for each category covered, so variety beats ten churches.
- **Against the class:** the sum becomes a percentile in the frozen class reference, so a village with a full day is compared with villages, not with Paris.
- **Explanation** in days, checked against `place.visit_h` already in the wire: "Two full days, strongest in museums and food."

B and D do not overlap: B is what you see within 5 km, D is what you can do within 30 minutes.

#### R, recognition (10%): what do independent bodies say?

| Register | Weight | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UNESCO World Heritage, site in or at the place | 1.00 | Only counted here now |
| Most Beautiful Villages federations | 0.95 | Only counted here now |
| Historic town registers | 0.72 | As now |
| Spa town registers | 0.68 | As now |
| National park the place lies in or borders | 0.62 | As now |
| UNESCO Global Geopark | 0.30 | New, was filed as national park (Bochum) |
| UNESCO Biosphere Reserve | 0.25 | New, was filed as national park |

- **Rule:** the best register counts in full; each extra one closes 0.18 × its weight of the remaining gap, so five weak labels never beat one strong one.
- **Explanation** names the registers: "UNESCO World Heritage (historic centre) and Plus Beaux Villages de France." A place with none says so, and R is 0; it is a bonus, not a penalty.

### The rubric: how every place gets an appeal score

The hand score becomes a written rubric with four anchor levels per class, and Claude scores every place in Claude Code sessions from a fact card (class, population, designations, top POIs by kind, beauty parts, nearby nature, Wikivoyage status, crowd level). No API: about 150 places per session, so the 2,298 without a hand score take roughly 15 sessions, and you check a random 5% per batch.

| Score | Band | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9.0 to 10 | Exceptional | People plan a trip around it; among the very best of its class in Europe |
| 8.3 to 8.9 | Excellent | Worth adding a day or driving an hour for |
| 7.6 to 8.2 | Very good | A strong choice to stay a few nights or build a day around |
| 6.9 to 7.5 | Good | A good base or a rewarding half day |
| 6.0 to 6.8 | Pleasant | Nice if you pass; fine for a night |
| under 6.0 | No badge | Fine to sleep or change trains; not a reason to go |

Each score is written with its reason sentence ("A walled hill town above the Val d'Orcia, with three Romanesque churches and almost no traffic"), so the reason exists for every place, not only the hand-scored ones. Translations are done in the same session, never at load time.

### Tiers

- **Score and band, both visible.** Cards show the number with one decimal and its band word, "8.9 Exceptional", plus the rank inside the class: "Top 2% of villages in Europe" (from `class_percentile`, already in the wire). Five bands give more distinction than three while staying inside the six levels people can tell apart.
- **One word set everywhere.** The existing "Very good / Excellent / Exceptional" set (`score.band1-3`) is extended with Good and Pleasant; the "Worth a visit / detour / journey" set goes.
- **Estimated scores** (a new place not yet rubric-scored) show as "about 8.1", marked "estimated", and are capped at Excellent.
- Hidden gem stays as a badge (Very good or better with low fame within its class), separate from the band.
- Band shares are guarded per class: Exceptional 0.5% to 4%, Excellent under 12%, so each word keeps its meaning.

### One row per city

Airports stop being destinations. FCO and CIA become `gateways` of Rome, so Rome appears once in every top list, and the flight search keeps reading the gateways.

## Layer 2: tiers for nature, labels for POIs

Trails, beaches, lakes, mountains and cycling routes get the same score and five bands as places, set Europe-wide within each kind, plus the facts people decide on; POIs get no number, only three labels with a cap per place.

### Nature items: one ladder, honest facts

- **Same scale as places.** A score with Exceptional, Excellent, Very good, Good or Pleasant. One ladder means a traveller learns it once.
- **Europe-wide within the kind.** Today trails are stretched per country and cycling per region, so the best trail in Flanders scores like the best in the Dolomites. v5 places each item on a frozen Europe-wide percentile within its kind: top 3% Exceptional, next 9% Excellent, next 18% Very good, next 25% Good, next 25% Pleasant.
- **Best nearby stays visible.** A rank line, "Best walk in Flanders" or "#2 of 41 lakes in Carinthia", so a local leader still leads its region's page without pretending to be Alpine.
- **No renormalising.** A missing part counts at the kind's median and lowers confidence; an item with fewer than half its parts measured is listed without a score. Today a beach with two known parts can outscore a beach with eight.
- **Facts are facts, not scores.** Difficulty and certification sit next to the score and never inside it.

| Kind | Tier from | Facts shown next to it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trails | Scenery, relief, shape, prominence, designation, photos (`trails/rate.py` weights kept) | Easy / Moderate / Hard mapped to the SAC scale, km, climb, hours, loop or one way |
| Beaches | Setting, water, sand, wildness, space, acclaim | EU bathing water class, Blue Flag as a badge, sand or pebble, lifeguard |
| Lakes | Scenery, swimming, activity, water, wildness | Swimming allowed, water class, shore access |
| Mountains | Scenery, access, stature, experience, views | How you get up (lift, hike, drive), difficulty, season |
| Cycling | Scenic, safety, surface, designation, services | Paved share, traffic-free share, climb, family suitable |

The match from layer 3 uses these facts directly: a family sees easy loops first; someone who picked hiking sees the Hard ridge first.

### POIs: three labels, no score

Scoring 157,775 POIs on a ten-point scale would be false precision. Each destination instead labels a few of its own POIs:

| Label | Rule | Cap per place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Must-see | Top significance within the place, and at least national recognition (heritage listing, Wikipedia in several languages) | Metro 8, city 5, town 3, village 2, area 3 |
| Recommended | Next band of significance, or a must-see that is not open in the chosen month | Up to 3 times the must-see cap |
| Worth a look | Any other rated POI | No cap |

- The caps fix today's fault, where 62% of POIs carry the top rate against a 12% quota (the last run used `--sig-only` and never re-quota'd).
- One category set across the app: sights, museums, nature, food and drink, active, family, nightlife, markets and shops. It replaces the four regex buckets in `planner/dayDraft.js:140`.
- Practical tags from roadmap 2.17 and 2.23 ride alongside: good with kids, step-free, indoors (rain), free.
- Within each label, POIs are ordered by the traveller's likes, which is all the personal layer does for POIs.

## Layer 3: the personal match

One taste profile per traveller, asked in four skippable questions, gives every place a match percentage and a bar for each thing the traveller picked. The match reorders lists and shows why; it never changes the place's own score.

### The taste profile

It reuses keys the app already has, so the planner, Explore and Ask Carta read one profile instead of three.

| Question | Answers | Existing key it reuses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who usually comes? | Solo, couple, friends, family (kids' ages), group | `PARTY_KINDS` in `planner/flow/tripSettings.js:27` |
| What do you go for? (up to 3, with photos) | beach, hiking, lakes, culture, city, food, nightlife, hidden, cycling, ski, wellness, islands | `LIKES` in `tripSettings.js:43`, the same keys as `countryMatch` |
| Where do you like to stay? | Big cities, towns, villages and countryside, a mix; and lively or quiet | New: `size` and `quiet`, read against the place class from layer 1 |
| How do you travel? | The Lifestyle preset (Backpacker to Foodie) and no car | `choices.lifestyle`, `AVOID` |

Every question has "No preference", which gives that dimension weight 0. A profile with nothing set means no fit is shown anywhere and lists sort by quality, as today.

### Where it is asked

- **First visit:** the Explore start dialog (`browse/StartDialog.jsx`) gains a "Tell Carta what you like" path of four screens, built from the planner's existing `g:likes` and Who screens. Skip is always one tap.
- **Everywhere after:** one chip in the Explore header, "For you: hiking, villages, quiet", opens the same four questions as a sheet. Account gets a "Your travel style" row beside Lifestyle.
- **Trip planner:** "Help me choose" starts prefilled from the profile and offers "Save as my travel style" at the end. It never overwrites the profile silently.
- **Learning, only with consent:** when someone saves five places that share a facet they did not pick, Carta asks once: "You save a lot of lake places. Add lakes to your style?"

Stored in `choices.taste`, so it rides the existing local storage (`lib/urlState.js`) and, when signed in, `user_settings.settings` (`hooks/useAccountSync.js`). No new table, and the GDPR export already covers it.

### The place profile

The pipeline gives every destination one value from 0 to 1 per like key. It starts from the 20 vibe scores already in public/search/guided.json and checks each against facts it already has: beaches and their tier within 20 km, trails and lakes within 30 minutes, UNESCO and heritage POIs for culture, food recognition and market density, bars and clubs for nightlife, cycling routes, ski areas, spas and thermal baths, island status, and `hidden_gem` with low fame for "hidden". Each value is a percentile against the frozen reference set, so it means the same in Bochum and in Bergen. Size class, crowd level by month, car need and cost level come along as they are.

### The formula

```latex
F = \frac{\sum_k w_k \, p_k}{\sum_k w_k} \times \prod_j c_j
```

`w` is 1 for each chosen like (0 otherwise), plus 0.5 each for the size and quiet answers; `p` is the place's value for that key; each `c` is a conflict factor between 0.6 and 1 (busy in the chosen month for a quiet traveller, car needed for a no-car traveller, over 30 °C for someone avoiding heat).

```latex
S_{\text{for you}} = Q + \lambda \, (F - 0.5), \qquad \lambda = 2
```

`Q` is the quality score, or the month score when a month is set. With λ = 2, the match moves a place by at most one point in the "For you" order, so a great-match Very good place can rise above a poor-match Excellent one, but the match can never make a weak place look strong. λ and the conflict factors live in `config/rating.json` and are tuned on the persona set (robustness section).

### How it reads

The match is shown as a percentage, 100 × F, from 55% up; below that no match is shown. Its data shows in two sizes:

- **On cards and map pins:** "86% match" next to the score, with the strongest reason: "86% match: hiking, quiet village".
- **On the overview and destination page, "Why it matches you":** one bar per thing the traveller picked, each with the fact behind it, and every conflict as its own line:

```
86% match for you

Hiking     ████████░░  14 trails within 30 min, 3 rated Excellent
Quiet      ██████░░░░  calm except August
Food       ███████░░░  2 markets, local wine
Village    ██████████  village, 780 people

Needs a car: no train within 20 km
```

- Each bar is the place's value for that like (`p` above), so the bars and the percentage always agree; the fact line comes from the same data that made the value.
- The score and the match sit side by side and never merge: "8.9 Exceptional · 86% match".
- Hard needs stay filters, never weights: kids under 3, no car, a budget ceiling.
- POIs get no match number; within each label (layer 2) they are simply ordered by the traveller's likes.

## Robustness: how it stays right

Everything is calibrated once on a frozen reference catalogue and guarded by eight gates in CI; a new place or lake is scored against that frozen frame, so it can never move anything else.

### Frozen, versioned, explicit

- One snapshot, `reports/rating_reference_v5.json`, holds every mean, spread, percentile breakpoint, class curve and weight. `zlog`, the fallback fit and the nature percentiles all read from it; nothing is refit on a normal run.
- Every rating carries `rating.version` ("v5.0"). Recalibrating is its own task with a before and after report, and bumps the version.
- All weights, cutoffs, caps and λ live in `config/rating.json`, the way prices moved to `config/pricing.json`, so the pipeline and the app read the same numbers.

### Eight gates, in CI

| # | Gate | Passes when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Stability | Adding 100 held-out places (and 100 items per nature kind) moves no existing score more than 0.05 and changes no tier |
| 2 | Golden pairs | 200 pairs, the 120 today plus village-versus-city and nature pairs: at least 95% in the right order |
| 3 | Fair across classes | Every class has 0.5% to 4% in the top tier; correlation of score with log population under 0.10 (kept) |
| 4 | Every tier explained | No tiered item without a reason and a confidence |
| 5 | One row per place | No two rows share a city; airports only as gateways |
| 6 | External check | Rank correlation of the top 200 against Tripadvisor Travellers' Choice, Michelin Green Guide and UNESCO counts; the 30 largest gaps judged by Claude with sources, checked by you (roadmap 2.10, decision T469-d) |
| 7 | Personas | 12 travellers (family with a toddler, hiking couple, friends going out, slow culture, backpacker, beach family, cyclist, foodie, skier, wellness, island hopper, no-car rail): Claude judges each top 10 with sources, at least 80% sensible, you check three |
| 8 | Contract | Schema bump to v19 with the new keys pinned to the second level (closes T082-d) |

### Adding a new destination, lake or trail

1. The intake adds the place (never without you, as now) and the pipeline assigns its class.
2. The pipeline builds its fact card and scores B, D and R against the frozen frame.
3. Until it is rubric-scored, its appeal is estimated from the frozen fit, marked "estimated" and capped at Worth a detour.
4. The next rubric session scores it from the fact card, with its reason sentence in six languages.
5. Gates 1 to 5 run; the place enters the wire with its tier, reason, confidence and taste profile.
6. A nature item skips step 4: its tier comes from its measured parts and the frozen Europe-wide percentiles of its kind.

Tiers are re-checked once a year in a review session, and a place can lose a tier, as Michelin stars can.
